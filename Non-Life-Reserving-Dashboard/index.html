Methods

Chain Ladder. Volume-weighted development factors, applied to the latest diagonal.

fj = Σi Ci,j+1 / Σi Ci,j    Ĉi,J = Ci,I−i · fI−i · … · fJ−1

London Chain (Benjamin & Eagles). Adds an intercept to each development step, fitted by least squares; useful when development is not proportional. Columns with fewer than 4 points fall back to the Chain Ladder factor.

Ci,j+1 = λj · Ci,j + αj

Taylor arithmetic separation. Splits incremental payments per claim into a development effect and a calendar-year (inflation) effect. Future calendar indices are extrapolated with a log-linear trend on the last 15 diagonals, which gives an explicit claims-inflation estimate.

Xi,j / ni = rj · λi+j,   Σ rj = 1

Mack (1993). Distribution-free mean squared error of the Chain Ladder reserve per accident year and in total (including covariances). The total reserve is then modelled as log-normal with the Mack mean and standard error to read off the 99.5% quantile, the Solvency II calibration level.

σ̂j² = 1/(I−j−1) · Σi Ci,j (Ci,j+1/Ci,j − fj)²

Validation. The Mack implementation reproduces the published Taylor–Ashe benchmark (Mack 1993): total reserve 18,680,856 and standard error 2,447,095.

Simulated data. Claim counts grow by a set rate; payments follow a fixed development pattern, calendar-year inflation and log-normal noise. Because the inflation used to generate the data is known, you can check how well the Taylor method recovers it.
